Drying a basement is its own challenge: the space is below ground level, air barely circulates and dampness lingers. Concrete block walls, poured concrete floors and the soil around them trap moisture and resist natural evaporation. Our restoration technicians understand these challenges and bring specialized equipment: submersible pumps for deep water, truck-mounted extraction units for large volumes, and dehumidifiers and air movers positioned for below-grade drying.

We also look for the underlying reason the basement took on water, because fixing the cause helps stop the next flood. To find contributing factors, we check grading, window wells, the sump pump, penetrations through the walls and the drainage at the foundation. From there you get our recommendations for lasting fixes, which may include waterproofing, interior drainage or installing a backup sump pump to guard the basement against the next intrusion.

Basement Water Removal Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Keep window wells clear of debris and consider adding window well covers
  • Maintain exterior grading that slopes away from your foundation
  • Place leak sensors by the water heater and sump pit so you hear about water early
  • Extend each downspout so the water lands 6 feet or more from the foundation
  • Use hydraulic cement to close any cracks you can see in the basement floor or walls
  • Consider an interior French drain system if seepage is a chronic issue

When to Call for Basement Water Removal in Brooklyn

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Stained or deteriorating carpet, tile or other basement flooring
  • Damp or wet walls, especially along the lower part of the foundation
  • Sump pump trouble: it never shuts off, never kicks on or sounds wrong
  • Corrosion at the bottom of appliances, shelving legs or steel columns
  • Peeling paint or soft, crumbling drywall on finished basement walls
  • Standing water on your basement floor, whatever the source
  • Efflorescence, a chalky white residue, forming on masonry or concrete
