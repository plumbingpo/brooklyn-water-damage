Right Now

Before We Arrive

  1. Cut the Power or GasFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • Drain valves that crack, work loose or stop sealing completely.
  • Excess pressure in the tank from a failed thermostat or a missing expansion tank.
  • Drain pans beneath closet or attic units that rust through or clog and then spill over.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Stand and Subfloor Damage

Under the tank, the wood stand or the subfloor absorbs the largest share of the leak. That wood may rot and lose strength, and it will have to carry the weight of a new tank filled with water.

Heater Closet Drywall

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.

Adjacent Room Flooring

Water leaving a heater closet runs under the door and into carpet, laminate or hardwood in the hall. Heat makes it worse, with quicker swelling of wood boards and carpet backing that comes apart.

Stored Belongings

Whatever is kept by a closet or garage heater, from keepsakes to furniture and boxes, is the first thing to get wet. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.

Will Insurance Pay?

What About Insurance?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost

  • Whether the platform or subfloor under the tank has to be rebuilt before a new heater goes in.
  • Flooring type in the neighboring rooms, where the water reached them.
  • Placement of the unit, as leaks from an attic or an inside closet run higher than those in a garage.
  • Quantity of insulation and drywall, on walls or ceilings, that must come out.
