The Science of Structural Drying in Brooklyn, NY

Home / Blog / The Science of Structural Drying in Brooklyn, NY

Ask people around Brooklyn, NY what restoration involves and drying the structure rarely comes up, although it is the core of the work. Pumping out the standing water still leaves a great deal of moisture inside the insulation, concrete, subfloor, wood framing, and drywall. Left in place, that moisture ends in odors, mold, and materials that break down. Below is a plain look at the science professionals rely on, and at why their results are much better than what DIY drying achieves.

What Psychrometry Means for Drying

Professional structural drying is based on psychrometry, the study of the physical and thermodynamic properties of gas-vapor mixtures. For a drying job, that comes down to knowing how relative humidity, airflow, and temperature combine to move moisture out of a wet structure. With psychrometric calculations, an IICRC-certified technician works out the setup for a given job: how many dehumidifiers and which type, where the air movers go, and what temperature and humidity to aim for.

Commercial Drying Equipment

Consumer dehumidifiers and fans do not compare to the machines a restoration crew brings. An hour of run time takes 15 to 30 or more pints out of the air with one LGR (Low-Grain Refrigerant) dehumidifier, against 2 to 3 pints with a household model. High-velocity air movers create precisely directed airflow at 2,500 CFM or more to speed evaporation from wet surfaces. Desiccant dehumidifiers are used for specialized situations, including cold-weather drying and extreme moisture loads. All of it costs tens of thousands, and using it well takes specific training.

How a Drying Job Unfolds

A professional drying job is done in a set order. The opening step is a full assessment: thermal imaging and meters locate and map each wet area. Technicians use the map to place equipment and to record starting moisture levels as a baseline. The crew then sets air movers and dehumidifiers where the psychrometric math says they will evaporate and capture the most moisture. Temperature may be raised to increase evaporation rates. A technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment as needed, and document progress. Drying continues until all affected materials reach acceptable moisture levels compared to dry reference standards.

What to Expect for Drying Time

Most residential structural drying projects take 3 to 5 days with proper commercial equipment. The timeline depends on several factors: the volume of moisture present, the types of materials affected (concrete dries much slower than drywall), ambient conditions, building construction, and the number and type of equipment deployed. As a rule, standard drywall needs 2 to 3 days, wood framing 3 to 4 days, and concrete 5 to 7 days or more. After the first moisture readings, the technician gives you an estimated schedule and updates it as the job moves along.

The Reason We Monitor Every Day

The difference between a professional job and guessing is monitoring every day. Nobody can say that materials are drying, keeping to the expected rate, or safely dry unless a calibrated instrument has measured them. Surfaces can feel dry to the touch while holding dangerous moisture levels deep within the material. Only calibrated moisture meters can provide this verification. There is a paperwork benefit too: documented drying proves to an insurance company that the job met industry protocol, and that backs up your claim and your home's value.
