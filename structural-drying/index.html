Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

The structural drying technicians at Brooklyn Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Different materials (drywall, hardwood, concrete, plaster) dry at different rates and need different approaches to reach acceptable moisture levels without causing additional damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

Drying a structure properly takes science, and guesswork has no place in it. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. To do it professionally, you have to know how each material takes on and gives up moisture, work out the grain depression, meaning the moisture removal capacity, for the job, and check progress every day. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Between the investment in equipment and the training needed to run it, it is no surprise that DIY drying falls well short of a professional job.

Maintenance Tips for Structural Drying

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Until a technician removes the equipment, keep it running day and night without breaks
  • The sooner drying starts after water gets in, the better things turn out, so do not wait
  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • Avoid moving the machines yourself, because their placement follows our moisture map
  • Daily monitoring only works if someone can let the technician into the property
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once

When to Call for Structural Drying in Brooklyn

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
  • Signs of deterioration where an old leak was left to dry on its own
  • A stale, musty smell that lingers even though the water was extracted
  • High humidity in affected areas despite efforts to ventilate
